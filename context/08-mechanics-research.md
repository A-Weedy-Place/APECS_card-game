# Mechanics research and design debate

Research date: 2026-10-13. This is the rationale for the next rules revision, targeting fourteen decisions, not a claim that every proposal below is implemented or balanced. Read `02-game.md` for implemented rules and `06-progress.md` for verified checks. All costs, yields, approval probabilities and social effects remain designer assumptions.

Verification scope: game rules and developer material were read or searched; no claim is made that the researchers personally completed commercial-game runs. The Civilization PDF was too large for direct web extraction; its indexed prerequisite section was readable. Exact Eureka/Inspiration percentages were not verified and are not relied upon. The analogy is conditional preparation and visible progression, not an assertion that the games use identical systems.

## Sectors are not the same as enabling resources

The existing colour-pair bonuses make many choices additive. Progression can instead follow concrete systems, while politics, economy, awareness and justice affect delivery across those systems. Colour remains a useful visual label, not a substitute for an interaction.

| Candidate pathway | Example progression | Cross-synergy or distinct purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Clean power | Solar/wind → grid and storage → managed coal exit | A cleaner, reliable grid improves later electrification. Worker support reduces disruption from retirement. |
| Buildings and mobility | Efficiency → clean heat/transit → deep retrofit | Efficiency has an early economic benefit; electrification benefits from cleaner power. |
| Industry and materials | Innovation → clean production → circular industry | High initial investment, delayed returns. Procurement and clean power strengthen the payoff. |
| Food and ecosystems | Community stewardship → restoration/farming → durable landscape protection | Land rights, participation and funding support implementation. Not an unlimited substitute for other mitigation. |
| Methane and waste | Monitoring → leak repair/waste recovery → stronger standards | Monitoring creates a targeted, affordable action route and supports later regulation. |
| Adaptation and resilience | Early warning → frontline protection → resilience compact | Protects people and economic capacity from disruption; does not automatically gain transition or cooling points. |

These are candidate groupings rather than six compulsory three-card ladders. Fourteen decisions should permit a few developed routes plus support choices. Alternate predecessors and shorter branches can prevent one exact sequence becoming mandatory. Politics, finance and public participation may also have their own upgrades, but their value should come from helping other actions work.

Specific proposed interactions include a grid bonus for electrification; procurement discounts for industry; worker protection against coal-exit disruption; participation support for land and transport projects; and public communication making warning systems more useful. Numerical bonuses require balance testing, not scientific attribution.

## Climate rationale and limits

[IPCC WGIII Chapter 13](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-13/) supports considering packages of policies and enabling institutions, finance, innovation and behaviour together. This motivates cross-card effects. It does not supply game approval odds, decay rates or a universal government decision model.

[IPCC WGIII Chapter 6](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-6/) describes complementary roles for clean electricity, electrification, storage and system integration. A power-to-buildings/industry connection is therefore a reasonable qualitative abstraction. Actual effects vary with technology, location and implementation; game points are not emissions estimates.

[IPCC WGIII Chapter 7](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-7/) identifies context, tenure, stakeholder needs and trade-offs in land-based mitigation. Nature cards should have financing and implementation constraints, but neither universal political opposition nor automatic large economic losses. Restoration is not equivalent to indiscriminate tree planting.

Justice means who receives benefits, who bears costs, whose voice counts, and whether vulnerable people and affected workers are protected. [IPCC WGIII Chapter 17](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg3/chapter/chapter-17/) discusses social protection, decent work, energy access and participation. In the game, justice can protect support during disruptive changes and recognise access/protection outcomes. It should not be reduced to pleasing politicians or treated as direct cooling.

Coal exit does not necessarily reduce overall growth. A plausible scenario is temporary disruption in an economy dependent on coal when replacement capacity and worker support are missing. Preparation can reduce that disruption and later investment can restore capacity. This conditional scenario is a game abstraction, not a universal economic law. The same caution applies to any economy-to-politics feedback: material conditions can affect support, but the relationship is neither automatic nor quantified by these sources.

[IPCC WGII Chapter 17](https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/chapter-17/) discusses actionable warnings, effective institutions and reducing harm. This supports a resilience role that protects people and economic capacity. Adaptation and loss-and-damage finance must remain distinct from mitigation; no automatic temperature or transition reduction follows from them.

## Debate and decisions

The game-design and climate reviewers agreed that sequencing, scarce credits and conditional synergies create useful difficulty. They challenged increasing random failure as a substitute for depth, compulsory politics-first openings, unconditional mitigation penalties and prerequisite chains starved by random offers.

There was an explicit disagreement about resolution. Deterministic readiness with bounded implementation setbacks was proposed as a stronger basis for a skill-ranked leaderboard. The competing argument is that the user explicitly wants approval probabilities, including very poor odds when support collapses. The chosen direction retains **actual stochastic approval** for contentious policies and shows the real odds and rejection cost before commitment. Reliable preparation/recovery and a free save option must prevent a failed roll from making recovery itself practically impossible. This decision does not establish competitive fairness.

Recommended safeguards for the implementation:

- Deal a progression opportunity, an enabler and a wildcard where eligible; prioritise successors not previously offered. A purchased foundation should produce accessible future choices.
- Keep low-cost preparation eligible late. Ambitious policies can have very low odds; basic recovery should remain reliable.
- Increase paid redraw costs visibly. Reviewers initially proposed escalation across the run; the implementation direction uses 1, 2, 3… within each turn, resetting next turn. Exact limits belong in `02-game.md`. Test whether repeated cheap first redraws make finding combos too easy.
- Preserve a free Skip/save choice at zero credits. Paid redraw must not be the only route out of an unaffordable hand.
- Give investment a delayed breakeven point. Cap repeated triggers and check money generation for positive feedback loops.
- Show forthcoming shared disruptions before they resolve, with more than one useful preparation route. Surprise random disasters would add another source of score luck.
- Expose prerequisites, discounts, conditional effects and protection in the card library. Keep the main screen short and readable.
- Use sources to justify qualitative relationships only. No simulator integration, forecast accuracy or empirical calibration has been established.

## Verification still required

Research is complete enough to guide a prototype; implementation and balance are separate work. Compare several purposeful strategies, a greedy baseline and random play on shared seeds. Inspect score distributions, low-cash turns, available successors, approval at low/high support, recovery time, reroll value and maximum achievable scores. More score variance alone is not evidence of better skill discrimination.

Human playtests must establish readability, enjoyable recovery and session length. Competition fairness remains pending: random offers and approval rolls can determine placement even when accurately disclosed. Shared scenarios, seed handling and a prize policy need separate evaluation. Any eventual claim that the new mechanics are implemented or tested must be backed by the progress log and checks, not this research note.

## Independent implementation review, 2026-10-13

The subsequent 0.4 engine review found two concrete issues: most synergy recipes were hidden until activated, and the guaranteed progression slot excluded adaptation and other enabling successors. Both were sent to the engine author and corrected: `synergyLibrary` exposes recipes, and the successor slot now includes eligible higher-tier cards regardless of action/enabler role. A separate 1,000-seed check after the correction offered adaptation on its first eligible turn in every case where early warning was the only prior policy.

Independent read-only checks also covered 273 accepted card/state cases across early, event and final turns. Displayed cost, approval, transition, resilience and effects matched committed receipts; final actions did not generate another income payment. A failed industrial action did not unlock its successor or charge upkeep. At zero credits/economy/support/awareness, Skip restored enough credits for the reliable campaign recovery. Sample late ambitious approval ranged from 5% without preparation to 76% in a supported state. Income was bounded at 6 in the reviewed implementation. These are correctness checks, not evidence that the game is perfectly balanced or prize-ready.

The land route's higher financing burden compared with the food route was challenged during review. Stewardship now provides a specific advantage, but relative route strength still needs simulation and human testing. Rules, test totals and any later tuning remain authoritative in `02-game.md` and `06-progress.md`.

## Independent 0.5 package review, 2026-10-13

The ten rounds are a 2026 policy-drafting session, not ten physical implementation periods. [En-ROADS dynamics](https://docs.climateinteractive.org/projects/en-roads/en/latest/guide/background.html) distinguish adopting incentives from replacing vehicles, buildings and energy infrastructure. The [renewables guide](https://docs.climateinteractive.org/projects/en-roads/en/latest/guide/renewables.html) also describes phased price incentives. These support separate adoption, model-start and implementation timing; they do not validate game negotiation fatigue or budget tranches. The more detailed numerical mapping audit belongs in `09-enroads-mapping.md`.

The package reviewer challenged intra-package ambiguity and resource exploits. Resolution now preflights distinct current offers and the whole upfront cost; quotes are frozen against the displayed state. The server orders selected cards by the hand, so changing request-array order cannot manipulate resource caps. Invalid members fail the whole transaction. Individual plays pay neither allocation nor maintenance; only explicit End round advances the round. An attempted policy, including a rejection, removes the empty-round saving bonus. Repeatable recovery cannot manufacture distinct-card family perks.

There is a deliberate consequence of frozen quotes: playing preparation first, then another card in a separate commitment, can improve that later card. Selecting both in one package uses the original quotes. This is visible sequencing, not a claim of automatic package synergy. It needs a short UI explanation. No quotes should promise improvements from a success that has not happened yet.

Public numeric RNG samples were also removed after review: an omitted seed alone is insufficient if predictable PRNG samples and their salts remain exposed. Internal rolls remain stored for deterministic engine behavior; player responses expose probabilities and outcomes, not roll samples.

The adapted controller probe can make multiple plays per round, then explicitly end it. A first 250-seed comparison per strategy (1,750 games) used allocation base 2, cap 6. Means were random 438.9, burst 385.9, mixed 511.1, finance 514.4, nature 510.9, technology 511.3 and politics 510.7. Mixed averaged 12.7 attempts, 3.7 multi-card rounds and 2.3 empty rounds. Increasing the optional controller route bonus from 14 to 40 on 100 seeds changed route usage but did not create a large score gap between specialists. The controllers share a greedy evaluator, count only short prerequisite opportunities and do not plan complete multi-step combinations; similar means are not proof of identical routes or bad balance.

The reviewed funding adjustment raises allocation base to 3 and gross cap to 7, leaving costs, transition gains and score weights intact. A 100-seed comparison per strategy (700 games) then gave means random 478.1, burst 430.6, mixed 569.8, finance 578.4, nature 570.8, technology 572.6 and politics 575.1. Mixed made 14.1 attempts, passed 5 mitigation actions, reached 5 upgrades and used 4.2 multi-card rounds; empty rounds fell to 1.7. Its scores spanned 418–728, with 10th/90th percentiles 482/636. Prepared mixed play beat the random controller on 88% of paired seeds and the unsupported burst controller on 97%.

The recommendation was to retain the funding change because it permits more actual decisions and upgrades, rather than inflate point values. No tested heuristic reached the 150 transition target or 1,000 score, but that is not proof that perfect play is impossible. Remaining concerns are high end-game economy values (roughly 94–97 for prepared controllers), heavy use of reliable resilience cards, random failures, human session duration and the strategic cost of committing a package before preparation. Human tests and a stronger lookahead controller are still needed before declaring competitive balance solved.
