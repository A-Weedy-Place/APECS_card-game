# Game purpose and requirements

An APECS member proposed a short interactive game for the COP31 Cryosphere Pavilion. The user reports that the proposal was approved. The chosen focus is Overshoot and Feasible Pathways to 1.5°C Emissions Reductions. Earlier Ocean Pavilion ideas are background; this implementation targets the cryosphere activity.

Visitors scan a QR code from a shared leaderboard screen and play in their phone browsers. A nickname, country or territory and final score appear on the public board after consent. The intended experience is entertaining and understandable without climate science expertise. It is not a technical workshop or an educational quiz that visitors must study for.

## Confirmed structure

- Ten planning rounds, all in 2026: eight policy rounds and special rounds 4 and 7. Saved 0.5 keeps repeated plays with explicit End round; 0.4 keeps fourteen dated decisions; 0.1–0.3 keep seven.
- No deck-building inventory. Three random eligible policies arrive in a policy round. Players tap to select up to three within budget and confirm one package, which settles the round.
- Prerequisites unlock upgrades within the same planning year; there are no new-game calendar or minimum-round technology locks. Mitigation remains available from the outset.
- Starting credits, income, saving, political support, awareness and economy create trade-offs.
- Political support and awareness influence approval; economy influences income.
- Include technology, politics, nature and climate justice pathways.
- Keep sessions around 3–5 minutes. No forced timer in the prototype.
- User authorised two short skill challenges on 27 October: accelerating flight and precision timing, with a selected resource reward. Challenges can be skipped. Three rare cards add costly upsides and surprise numerical downsides.
- Run locally now; deployment remains a later user decision.
- Maintain indexed context notes so future work need not reread the entire conversation.

## Scientific and organisational boundaries

The further mechanics revision requests independent agent research/debate about small card engines, Slay the Spire and Civilization VI; deeper prerequisite paths and cross-card synergies; stronger scarce-budget/politics/economy interactions; costly escalating reshuffles; a browsable card library; and actual prototype playthroughs. Simple to understand, difficult to optimise. Approval can become very low for unsupported ambition, but recovery must remain possible. Greater score variance alone is not proof of skill or fairness. Expanded explanations belong in dialogs.

The user wants credible future outcomes and specifically asked to inspect En-ROADS controls and integrate their values into cards. Official control descriptions, units, ranges and timing now inform metadata; the chosen card targets remain designer assumptions. En-ROADS v26.9 begins inspected implementation inputs in 2027, which is distinct from adopting the package in 2026. No SDK/API access, temperature output or scientific validation is available by assumption. The prototype must not attribute custom social rules to those models. Approval does not imply endorsement of prototype rules, badges or any hosting commitment. Do not contact organisations or external speakers automatically.
